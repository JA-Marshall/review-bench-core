two of those with a should_fix expectation). Criteria: `criteria.json` as

## What happened on the three real defects

- **SecretStr non-ASCII TypeError (PR 13518).** Luna and Muse caught it in all
  three repeats, as blocking, with summaries that name `compare_digest` and
  the TypeError. Haiku never did; in two repeats it raised the subclass
  asymmetry of `isinstance(other, self.__class__)` instead.
- **Bare MutableSequence dispatched to the sequence schema (PR 13573).** Muse
  caught it three times out of three. Luna once out of three. Haiku never. This
  is the 700-line refactor; the defect is one entry in a 60-entry dispatch
  table and the reviewer has to know what `LIST_TYPES` used to contain.
- **Serialization format applied to validation-mode schemas (PR 13665).** Luna
  found it every time but called it blocking only once and should_fix twice,
  so it scores one hit out of three; that gap is what the lenient column
  shows. Haiku found it once, as blocking. Muse never mentioned it.

Every hit describes the expected defect; none is a keyword accident.

## The spurious blocking findings, adjudicated

Five blocking findings hit no expectation. All five are recorded as false
positives at blocking severity in the cases' `adjudications`, with the reason:

- Subclass-asymmetric equality on PR 13518 (Luna once, Haiku once): a real
  should_fix, no wrong record or crash in normal use.
- The mock core schema not being guarded on PR 11890 (Haiku twice): the mock
  is regenerated by the rebuild that follows; a should_fix question at most.
- Traversal not strictly linear on PR 13523 (Luna once): output unchanged;
  a complexity nuance.

The two should_fix expectations behaved as intended: Haiku named the
`hash(type(self.mode))` slip on PR 6414 twice at should_fix, and nobody found
the numeric-default contradiction on PR 13711, which no reviewer could have
seen from that diff without knowing how defaults are serialised.

## Compliance and cost

One Haiku call returned no JSON object; one Luna call exited without writing
its output file. Both count against compliance, which is the point of the
criterion: a gate that sometimes returns nothing is not a gate.

Luna read a lot: about 2.8M input tokens over 36 calls, roughly 77k per call,
almost all of it repository reads, for 64k output tokens. Haiku's reported
input excludes cache reads, so its figure is not comparable; it emitted about
570k output tokens. Muse reports no usage. Mean latency: Luna 43 s, Muse 118 s,
Haiku 159 s.

## What this does and does not show

- Muse is the best cheap gate on this batch: no false blocking findings at all
  and the highest recall, at the cost of missing the schema-mode defect
  entirely.
- Luna sees the most (lenient recall 0.78) but under-calls severity. If the
  loop treated should_fix as a soft block, Luna's numbers would look different;
  that is a policy choice the harness makes visible rather than decides.
- Haiku at this effort is not a reviewer for this kind of code.
- Three blocking expectations over three repeats is nine outcomes per backend.
  The intervals in the full table are 0.27 to 0.81 for Luna and 0.35 to 0.88
  for Muse; they overlap. Nothing here ranks Luna against Muse.
- All three seeded pull requests merged in July and August 2026 and their fixes
  are public. Whether any of these models saw them in training is not known.
- The rulings behind the cases were drafted by an AI assistant from the
  evidence in each `provenance.json` and are marked pending review by the
  repository owner. Treat the corpus as a worked example until that review
  has happened.
